What policies can RL learn that IL cannot?

This might sounds like a very naive question.

Policy optimisation

From an information-theoretic viewpoint, policy learning is all about doing optimisation to arrive at a useful policy. Usefulness here is understood as having utility

Conceptually we have two tools available to us,

  • The forward Kullback-Liebler (KL) divergence
  • The reverse KL divergence.

The distinction between forward- and reverse-KL is terminology that resides only the context of optimisation. One should think of π(⋅∣x)\pi(\cdot | x)π(⋅∣x) as the policy under optimisation

Imitation learning

In imitation learning, the learner is provided with trajectories from what is considered to be a expert policy π∗\pi^\astπ∗ and is trained to imitate the actions of the expert. This falls cleanly into the forward KL formulation.

Reinforcement learning

While we in the forward objective can compute the expectation using samples from π∗\pi^\astπ∗, in the reverse objective we do not have access to a closed-form expression of the probability density π∗\pi^\astπ∗. Typically in RL, we consider this density to be induced by a reward function R(x,y)∈RR(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}R(x,y)∈R. We can interpret RRR as the joint energy between a state and action. Ideally we would want (state, action)-pairs with high reward to be more probable, this is achieved through the Boltzmann distribution

pR(y∣x)=1Z(x)exp⁡(1βR(x,y))p_R(y|x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \exp \left ( \frac{1}{\beta} R(x,y) \right)pR​(y∣x)=Z(x)1​exp(β1​R(x,y))
