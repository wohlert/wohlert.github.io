In reinforcement learning with large language models it is common to use some sort of policy gradients objective to maximise a reward objective with an associated cost term that penalises deviations from a reference policy. This optimisation problem for a single datapoint xxx looks as follows where we wish to optimise the policy π\piπ,

max⁡πEy∼π(⋅∣x)[r(x,y)]−βDKL(π∣∣πref)\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot | x)} \left [ r(x, y) \right ] - \beta D_{\text{KL}}(\pi||\pi_\text{ref})πmax​Ey∼π(⋅∣x)​[r(x,y)]−βDKL​(π∣∣πref​)

Here r(x,y):X×Y→Rr(x, y): \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}r(x,y):X×Y→R is any reward function that takes the prompt xxx and the completion yyy and assigns a scalar.

We call this reinforcement learning, but is that really the right term for this?

We can compute the optimal policy

π∗(y∣x)=1Z(x)πref(y∣x)exp⁡(1βr(x,y))\pi^\ast(y|x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_\text{ref}(y|x)\exp\left ( \frac{1}{\beta} r(x,y) \right )π∗(y∣x)=Z(x)1​πref​(y∣x)exp(β1​r(x,y))

Where the normlisation constant ZZZ is prompt-dependent and defined as

Z(x)=Ey∼πref(⋅∣x)[exp⁡(1βr(x,y))]Z(x) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\text{ref}(\cdot|x)} \left [ \exp \left (\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y) \right ) \right ]Z(x)=Ey∼πref​(⋅∣x)​[exp(β1​r(x,y))]

and then we can define a new objective as finding the policy π\piπ in the space of policies that minimises the divergence to the optimal policy. With a bit of substitution we see that this gives us a bound that matches our original objective exactly (note that we are minimising instead of maximising so signs are reversed).

This direction is mode-seeking, i.e. the reverse KL.
